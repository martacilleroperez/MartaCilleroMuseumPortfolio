Segmentation, Reconstruction, and Communication: Applying Generative AI as a Tool for Cultural Analysis and Augmentation

Done in collaboration with the Digital Visual Studies, in the Univeristy of Zurich.

This bachelor's thesis explores how AI can support the research, reconstruction, and communication of non-photographic historical images. The images used were 18th-century engravings showcasing the Roman Carnival; they are unique for their artistic style and the information. they portray the crowd behaviour and the relationships among some of the figures in the festivities. For this, experimentation was conducted with multimodal AI, segmentation techniques, and diffusion-based image inpainting algorithms. This process showcased the different strengths and weaknesses of the algorithms. The research resulted in a web-based prototype that contains AI-assisted segmentation and image augmentation to communicate cultural heritage.
